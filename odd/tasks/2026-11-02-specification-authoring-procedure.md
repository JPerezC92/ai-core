## Specs

- S1 — Entry anatomy. Requirement: the skill must show the shape of one `S#` entry, not only describe it in prose. Acceptance: `### Write meaningful specifications` presents a labeled anatomy consisting of a numbered `S#`, an observable behavior or constraint statement, the requirement-defining wording preserved, and an acceptance clause; a reader can copy the shape to write a new entry.
- S2 — Splitting rule. Requirement: "One `S#` per independently verifiable outcome. Split when no single check covers all parts." Acceptance: the section states the one-outcome rule and the split condition, and the worked example splits a multi-part request into separate entries.
- S3 — Symptom handling. Requirement: "A symptom report is not yet a requirement: state the behavior you are committing to, and keep the symptom verbatim." Acceptance: the section states that rule, and the worked example keeps a reported symptom verbatim inside the entry that commits to the behavior.
- S4 — Acceptance-check phrasings. Requirement: "Acceptance names an action and an observable result. \"Works correctly\" is not acceptable." Acceptance: the section states the action→result rule and names the unacceptable generic phrasing; at least one example acceptance clause follows the form.
- S5 — Illustrative-label boundary. Requirement: the anatomy labels are illustrations, not required syntax. Acceptance: the section explicitly says the labels are illustrative and no consumer states or implies they are mandatory literals; the mechanical validator continues to accept any `- S# — …` entry.
- S6 — Preserved fidelity and conversation separation. Requirement: existing obligations must survive this revision unchanged — exact strings, errors, commands and examples stay verbatim; approvals and directions to analyze stay in Log; mixed messages keep the requirement in Specs and the full message in Log; no invented scope; uncertainty recorded as evidence or a focused question. Acceptance: a complete original-to-candidate accounting shows each existing clause retained or translated with authority and rationale, and the revised section still contains each one explicitly.
- S7 — Worked example. Requirement: the section keeps a correct example, a rejected example and a mixed-message example, and the correct example demonstrates the anatomy and acceptance form. Acceptance: all three example kinds are present and the correct one is internally consistent with S1/S3/S4.
- S8 — Consumer alignment. Requirement: the checklist and template must manifest the revised procedure without becoming a second policy definition. Acceptance: `references/_checklist.md` Create section gains a check for one acceptance action→result per entry and for splitting multi-part requests, pointing at the skill section; `odd/tasks/_template.md` illustrates the same anatomy with consistent quotation style; neither duplicates the full procedure.
- S9 — Scope boundary. Requirement: no mechanical enforcement is added. Acceptance: `feature_validation` rules and the validator remain anatomy-only and unchanged; the section and checklist state that semantic review, not the validator, owns whether an entry is meaningful. No new skill, tracker, configuration, permission, dependency, code or destination change is included.
